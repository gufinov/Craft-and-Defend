# Game concept

**Craft-and-Defend** is the working label for a first-person voxel survival fortress game. Earlier names include Block Castle Siege and the phrase From Forest to Fortress. Final branding is open. No derivation from the Fog of War board game is established; do not import board-game or MinionClash rules by association.

## Player fantasy

Begin alone in a hostile forest with empty hands and a short chance to establish shelter. Gather wood, make tools, obtain stone, build a workbench/furnace, smelt iron, reshape land and turn a vulnerable camp into a fortress. The player acts directly. Machines may later automate repeated work; allied worker/minion management is not part of the initial design.

Survive → gather → dig/build → craft → improve equipment → fortify → defend → expand capability. Defense gives building a purpose; exploration brings materials and discoveries. Construction must feel readable and satisfying before attacks are added.

## World and construction

- Finite region with depth, hills, trees, exposed patches and resources reached by excavation. A long rectangle with friendly and hostile ends is current direction, not a final dimension.
- Remove and fill terrain: level hills, clear trees, dig trenches/moats, quarry, mine and tunnel. The first test plane is a fixture, not the full world.
- A tunnel may later become an enemy route if discovered. Underground construction is not guaranteed immunity.
- Place blocks first. Later templates describe walls, towers, gates, battlements, mines and trenches; building consumes resources and player/machine effort.
- One logical occupant per cell. Thin traps still reserve cells; larger structures reserve all cells in their footprint. Simple workstations exercise the rule in Foundation.
- Original textures, objects and equipment; reference games inform design only.

## Threats and endgame

Longer progression moves from wildlife and raiders through dens/outposts, monsters, rifts and wizard pressure. Enemy capabilities differ: wolves pursue players/livestock; some larger animals may damage weak storage/wood; trained attackers and siege creatures may breach suitable materials. These are design examples, not implemented combat specifications.

The strongest current ending is **the wizard personally arrives with a final wave at the player's fortress**. Defeating him and surviving resolves the campaign. A boss expedition is not required. Endless mode, post-victory sandbox and alternate endings remain optional decisions.

Stationary ballistae, catapults, turrets and traps can create active defenses while the player fights and repairs. Friendly rolling siege machines, armies and RTS workers are deferred.

## Questions to test later

How can the player explore without arbitrary losses while away? Are major waves preceded by warnings, tied to time, or unlocked by milestones? What defense works unattended? What happens on death or fortress defeat? First test one warned attack and an excursion/return loop before specifying a campaign director.

Foundation proves app control, terrain, movement, gathering, crafting and recovery. It does not prove army-scale AI, campaign balance or commercial readiness. See [scope](PROTOTYPE_SCOPE.md).
