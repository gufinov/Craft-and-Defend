# Coaster rails — side project (slopes, the loop drag tool, the mine cart)

Owner idea (2026-09-19, verbatim intent): rails that climb 45 degrees so mine carts can go up mountains, and a "roller coaster" option — click-and-drag with keys held draws interesting shapes (a loop first; a DNA strand or corkscrew later): equip a loop rail, drag out the straight lead-in, hold Shift to start a loop, X / C make it smaller / bigger while the ghost re-sizes, release lays the loop with a trailing flat exit. Later the same rails may carry mine carts hauling ore.

This is a **modular side feature**: every new file carries a `coaster` name (`game/scripts/rails/coaster_rails.gd`, `game/scripts/rails/coaster_cart_service.gd`, `game/scripts/diagnostics/coaster_rails_automation.gd`, `TEST_COASTER_RAILS.cmd`, this contract and its evidence). The core game touches it in a handful of clearly commented lines; nothing in the core depends on it beyond the `CoasterRails.chain` call that reproduces the P4C rail chain for flat rails.

## Chain model (`CoasterRails`, `game/scripts/rails/coaster_rails.gd`)

Every placed track piece (`rail`, `rail_slope`, `rail_loop`) lists the cells it offers a joint to (`connections(record)`); two pieces are joined only when **each lists the other's anchor** (mutual rule). `chain(stations, start)` returns `{cell: Array[Vector3i] joined cells}` for the connected track; `connected_cells`, `track_records`, `ride_point`, `loop_offsets` support the visuals, the cart and the drag tool.

- **Flat `rail`**: its four same-level neighbours, plus the cell diagonally below in each direction (so a slope climbing up to it can join). Flat-only chains are exactly the P4C 4-neighbour chains — `SiegeDefenseService._rail_chain` now calls `CoasterRails.chain`, and `_rail_step` / `_chain_degree` / `_chain_end_farthest` read the joined cells from the chain instead of stepping fixed offsets, so kettles ride flat chains as before (T133 / T154 / T155 PASS) and additionally climb slopes (their turret sits half a cell higher over a slope, `_rail_point`).
- **`rail_slope`** at `a` rising toward `h`: low end → `a − h` (a flat rail) or `a − h − UP` (a same-direction slope one lower); high end → `a + h + UP` (a flat rail one up and one forward, or the next slope). A wall-top rail never joins a rail on the ground beside the wall (the lower one only lists cells one level *down*).
- **`rail_loop`**: its four same-level neighbours (so a lead-in joins ordinary rails) plus the joints recorded in its station record by the drag tool — `coaster_joints`, a JSON-safe list of `[dx, dy, dz]` offsets to the previous and next cell of the drawn path (`WorkstationService.try_place(..., extra)` merges them; `restore` keeps unknown fields, so saves round-trip). A loop piece placed by hand (no recorded joints) falls back to the eight in-plane neighbours of its rotation's vertical plane. Recorded joints are necessary because a loop's risers sit directly above its lead-in and exit cells, which pure adjacency would wrongly join.

## Loop drag tool (`InteractionService` mode `coaster_loop`)

Held item `rail_loop` → right-press starts the drag (`begin_coaster_loop_at`), like an entity line. Keys while dragging:

| Input | Effect |
|---|---|
| move the aim | stretches the flat lead-in along the dominant axis at the anchor's height (`set_drag_end`) |
| **Shift** (the Interact action) | adds the loop and **latches** it; while held the lead-in is frozen so dragging up does not stretch it |
| **X** / **C** | loop radius −1 / +1 within **2..6** (default **3**); one step per key press (edge-triggered) |
| release right mouse | commits every validated ghost cell (`COASTER_PLACED`); left press cancels as for any drag |

X and C are read as raw keys (`Input.is_key_pressed(KEY_X / KEY_C)`) by `GameSession._update_placement_preview` and handed to `interaction.coaster_loop_keys(x, c)` each frame; the service edge-triggers them. They are deliberately **not** InputMap actions: they only act during a loop drag, the keybind fixture / editor stay untouched, and X / C are unbound in the default map. Diagnostics drive `set_coaster_loop`, `resize_coaster_loop` and `coaster_loop_keys` directly.

Geometry: the loop is the ordered cells of a midpoint circle of the chosen radius in the vertical plane of the line (`CoasterRails.loop_offsets`), starting one cell past the lead-in with its bottom row at the lead-in's height, then a two-cell flat exit after the bottom row. Cell counts: radius 2 → 12 loop cells, 3 → 16, 4 → 24, 5 → 28, 6 → 32; a 4-cell lead-in with the default loop is 22 pieces. Every cell is validated with `WorkstationService.preview_placement` (air, loaded, not occupied, not the player); loop pieces need no support, so a coaster floats — accepted for the side project. The ghost reuses the P3J drag preview (green / amber / red cubes). `drag_state()` gains `loop`, `loop_radius`, `loop_cells`.

## The cart (`CoasterCartService`, `game/scripts/rails/coaster_cart_service.gd`)

A Node that `GameSession` adds only when the first `mine_cart` is placed and frees when the last one goes; `advance(delta, paused)` runs from `GameSession._process` beside the siege service. Each cart rides the chain under its own cell at `cart.rail_speed` cells/s (no gravity, constant speed) and turns around at each end. Traversal: pick the joined cell with the smallest turn from the arrival direction, never the cell it came from; a *curved* loop piece (a `rail_loop` with a joint on another level) that is unvisited and within a 90° turn wins over a flat continuation (so the cart climbs into the loop instead of running straight through the shared bottom row), while a curved piece already ridden this pass is shunned (so it leaves onto the exit); at a dead end it turns and forgets what it visited. The cart's up vector is the curvature normal on loop pieces (it leans in and hangs upside down over the top) and straight up elsewhere; the collision shapes ride along so dismantling aims at the cart. `rider_cell(id)` / `trail(id)` for diagnostics. The kettle's `_ride_rails` is not touched.

## Limits — what is NOT done

- No corkscrew / DNA strand / helix; only the vertical loop. No lateral offset: the loop shares its bottom row with the entry and exit, so it is a circle of cells, not a true self-crossing loop.
- Top-row loop pieces whose joints all lie flat draw as ordinary rail blocks (rails on top), so the cart hangs under a "floor" of rail blocks over the top.
- The lead-in and exit are `rail_loop` pieces (one item, as the owner described), not ordinary `rail` items.
- Slopes are placed one at a time with W / R for the rise direction; no slope drag. A slope needs support below; loop pieces never do (floating coasters are accepted).
- Kettles climb slopes but do not route through vertical or diagonal loop joints (their junction rule keeps stepping "straight" by offset).
- No gravity, momentum or speed change; no ore hauling, no cart inventory, no right-click panel for the cart.
- Placeholder icons and box-part models; no owner art yet. Pressing X / C outside a loop drag does nothing and they cannot be rebound.

## Loop snap on a foundation (owner 2026-09-20)

Owner method: lay the loop's **base** yourself — a straight row of rails with a **Rail Slope at each end rising outward** — then aim **Rail Loop** at the base and right-click: the arch snaps up in one go (`InteractionService._snap_loop_on_base`, `CoasterRails.loop_arch_offsets`). The loop's size is fixed by the base: with the slopes 2h cells apart the arch is the upper part of a circle of radius h + 1 whose vertical sides start at the slope tops (5 rails between the slopes → radius 4, 15 pieces, 7 high). The slopes must be an even number of cells apart (an odd count of rails between them) or the snap is refused (`LOOP_BASE_ODD`); the snap needs that many Rail Loop items and free air above. The arch pieces join in order and to the two slopes, so a cart circulates: base → right slope → up → over → down the left slope → base. Entry and exit are up to the builder: two Lane Switchers put the entry lane and the exit lane beside the base row (the car enters the base from the first switcher's exit heading toward one slope, and after the loop leaves the base through the second switcher's entry), e.g. row A → switcher → base row (the loop) → switcher → row C, the second switcher one column further along so the two never share a cell. Aiming Rail Loop anywhere else still starts the old drag tool. T169; sandbox foundation on row z=41 (`--coaster-sandbox-loop-snap-check`).

## The Climb (CoasterCraft card 5, 2026-09-20)

Slope-in, straight climb of any grade, slope-out — one piece. Item **Climb** (`rail_climb`, Workbench 212, 2 iron + 3 planks → 8; stack 64; `coaster_tool: "climb"`). Hold it and right-press at the entry cell (flat, at rail height): the ghost is the **whole climb** from the entry to a landing `length` cells ahead and `rise` cells up — or down: a negative rise is a **descent** — heading along the placement rotation the whole way. The profile is one `TrackCurve`: `TrackCurve.make_climb` = an `s_bend` with no lateral shift (smoothstep height blend, so the concave slope-in, the straight grade and the convex slope-out are one continuous curve) and **bank 0** — a vertical profile has no side to lean into, so `up_at` returns world up and the car pitches with its heading only (the seat view already pitches with the rig). `CoasterRails.climb_layout(entry, quarters, length, rise)` lays it as `rail_loop` records with the curve (`TrackCurve.pieces(curve, "rail_loop", rotation, entry − along, landing + along)`): the first cell is the entry, the last the landing = `entry + along × length + up × rise`, both at rail height on their levels (ride point y = cell.y + 0.55), so a plain rail behind the entry and one ahead of the landing (at the landing's level) join flush; every piece joins only its recorded neighbours.

Sizing: defaults length 8, rise 4 (3..60 and −30..30). **Shift held**: the entry stays put and the aim sets both — its distance ahead along the heading = length, its height above / below the entry = rise; aim at a hilltop and the landing sits on it, aim into a valley for a descent. **4–9 / X / C** set the rise; **W / R** turn it. Release lays all-or-nothing for **one Climb item per piece** (about length + |rise| pieces; the sandbox is creative: free); the pack caps the length (`InteractionService.climb_length_limit`, like the loop's diameter) and the world's ceiling / floor cap the rise (`climb_rise_limit`). A cell in the way (ground, tree, hill, block) shows red and nothing is laid (`CLIMB_BLOCKED`); unaffordable pieces show amber. Terrain is never dug by this tool (the auto-clear card is separate).

The mountain test (sandbox, plate z 62..68, lane 65 — clear of the spawn cell at (0, 40)): a 10-wide stepped stone bank (x −5..4, one step per cell up to 6 high), a climb of length 13 / rise 6 from x = −13 onto its flat top, three plain rails across, a descent of length 10 / rise −6 back to the plate and a mine cart riding up, across and down. Tests: T176 (tool), T177 (mountain), T178 (render `coaster-climb.png`).

In-game names follow the ids: **Rail Bend** and **Rail Cross**.

- **Crossing** - item `rail_cross` (order 210, 3 iron + 4 planks -> 4, stack 32), `coaster_tool: "cross"`. ## Flat curves: 90°, U-turn, free curve (CoasterCraft card 4, 2026-09-20)

The **Curve** item (`rail_curve`; Workbench order 211, 2 iron ingot + 3 planks → 8, stack 64; `coaster_tool: "curve"`) lays a whole flat arc of `rail_loop` pieces at once. Right-press at the entry cell: the ghost is `TrackCurve.make_arc(center, along, side, radius, 0, sweep)` with `center = entry ride point + side × radius`, so the first piece rides its own cell centre (rail height, y + 0.55) and the arc leaves in the entry heading and bends toward `side` = the RIGHT of travel by `sweep` degrees. Defaults 90° and radius 4. Controls while the ghost shows: **W / R** turn the entry heading; **4–9 / X / C** set the radius (2..30); **Shift held** aims the arc — the sweep snaps by the aim's angle from the travel direction (0..22.5° → 45°, ..67.5° → 90°, ..112.5° → 135°, beyond → 180°), aiming to the LEFT of travel mirrors the bend (`curve_left`, side flipped), and half the aim's distance is the radius. Release lays every piece or nothing (red = blocked, amber = the pack cannot pay); in the real game each piece costs one Curve item and the radius is capped by the pack like the loop (`InteractionService.curve_radius_limit`); creative (the sandbox) is free. Layout: `CoasterRails.curve_layout(entry, quarters, radius, sweep, left, along)` → `TrackCurve.pieces(curve, "rail_loop", rotation, entry − along, exit + exit_heading, {}, steps)`; the pieces float, ride and draw their curve like every `TrackCurve` piece, and are **banked** (`make_arc`'s bank 0.35): riders and the seat view lean toward the centre by about a third of the way (mid-piece up vector `(±0.34, 0.88, ±0.34)`), and the drawn rails tilt with them (`_build_loop_track_visual` now spreads the rails along the curve's binormal for every curve piece; loops are unchanged).

**Ends.** The exit heading is the end tangent snapped to the nearest grid step (`CoasterRails.heading_cell`): for 90° it is `side`, for 180° `−along` — an axis neighbour at rail height, so a plain rail joins each end flush (T173 / T174: three rails behind and three beyond chain through, carts ride through and back). A U-turn's exit lane is 2R to the right heading back. For **45° / 135°** the end tangent is diagonal and the exit's `after` cell is the diagonal neighbour (`Vector3i(round(tangent))`): a plain rail **cannot join a diagonal end yet** (its joints are axis-only; T174 checks that such a rail stays alone). Only another curve continues it: a Curve pressed on that diagonal cell takes the diagonal heading automatically (`InteractionService.curve_entry_heading` → `CoasterRails.curve_end_heading`; the drag state reports `curve_diagonal_entry`), so two 45s back to back make a 90° whose ends both join plain rails, and a 45° right + 45° left (Shift-aim left) make a smooth S one lane over. A diagonal join is bridged by a short straight between the two arcs' end pieces (the arc ends inside its last cell; the next arc starts at the next cell's centre) and an S ends about a third of a cell off the lane — the smooth lane switcher (card 2) is the exact tool for lane shifts.

Feedback: `CURVE_PLACED` / `CURVE_BLOCKED`; the on-screen hint when the Curve is selected explains the gesture. Sandbox: a radius-4 90° (entry (−12, 37) heading +x) and a radius-3 U-turn (entry (2, 36)) behind the loop on z 36..42, each with a mine cart on its approach; `--coaster-sandbox-facing-check` still 0 reversals. Tests: T173 (90°: ghost, Shift snapping incl. the left mirror, pack cap, all-or-nothing, flush ends, cart ride and lean, save round-trip), T174 (U-turn; 45 + 45 chaining with the diagonal join), T175 (`coaster-curves.png`).
### Track auto-clear (CoasterCraft card 6, 2026-09-20)

A global setting, off by default: pause menu **Track auto-clear: on/off** (next to Hero: Armour; `[track] auto_clear` in `settings.cfg`, `SettingsStore.track_auto_clear`, mirrored into `InteractionService.auto_clear` by the app / `GameSession.set_track_auto_clear`). When ON, every track lay tool - the loop element, the lane switcher, the rail line drag and a single rail / slope placement (`auto_clear_plan` in `preview_place_item`, `_replan_entity_line`, `_replan_loop_element`, `_replan_lane_switch`) - treats a cell blocked **only by clearable terrain** as clearable: the ghost shows it **amber-orange** (state `clear`, apart from the paler "unaffordable" amber and the red "blocked"), and on release the blocking voxels are mined first (`clear_cells_for_track`: `world.set_cell(cell, 0)`, the block's `drop` added to the pack as if mined; a full pack loses the drop), then the pieces are laid. The feedback line ends with "Cleared N blocks for the track." Clearable = natural terrain the pick could mine: grass, dirt, stone, sand / gravel, logs, leaves, ores. Never cleared: water, bedrock, any `protected` block, **castle stone and planks** (the world keeps no edit record, so these read as player-built wherever they stand), entity cells, and blocks propping another entity (`supported_by`). Only the piece's own occupied cells are cleared; a slope's support cell below is required, not created. Off, nothing changes: a blocked cell is red and refused as before (T179; `--p3d-usability-automation=phase1` regression).

### Automatic trestle supports (CoasterCraft card 8, 2026-09-20)

Every floating `rail_loop` piece (a record with `curve`, or a classic ring piece) grows a **support** when it is placed (`GameSession._add_track_supports`, called from `_build_rail_loop_visual`): from the piece's ride point a thin stone post (0.12 square, castle-stone material) straight down until it meets the first solid voxel (water is not solid) or another track piece's cell, capped at `SUPPORT_MAX_DROP` 24 cells (nothing found = no post); a gold stud caps the post under the rail, and posts taller than 4 get a diagonal brace every 4 cells (`SUPPORT_BRACE_EVERY`), like the slope's trestle posts. No post for **inverted** pieces (`TrackCurve.up_at` more than 90 degrees from world up - over the top of a loop the post would cross the ring) or for a piece whose post would pass through **another piece of the same curve within 2 cells** (the loop's sides, where the ring stacks). Posts live in a `Support` node under the piece's body (rebuilt with it, gone when dismantled) and have no collision. Laying or removing a track piece re-draws the loop pieces up to 24 cells above it in the same column (`_refresh_rail_neighbours`) so a post that landed on a later piece is re-cut. Terrain mined from under a post later does not re-draw it (visual only; the next rebuild of that piece fixes it). Sandbox: the premade loop shows its posts. T180 (gate), T181 (`coaster-supports.png`).

#### Trestle trusses (owner playtest 2026-09-20, item 7)

The one-post support read as "broken sticks" under a tall climb, so every supported piece now grows a real **bent** (`GameSession._support_bent` works out where it stands, `_add_track_supports` builds it): two stone legs 0.10 square, one under each rail (±0.32 across the piece's travel direction — the curve tangent projected flat, or the loop's plane axis for a ring piece), each on a 0.4 × 0.15 × 0.4 stone pad on the floor with a gold stud where it meets the rail. Between the legs, steel (`545a63`) sway bracing every `SUPPORT_BRACE_EVERY` (2) cells from the floor up: a horizontal tie and an X of two diagonals per panel, plus a cap tie just under the rails. Bents taller than 4 cells splay their legs outward toward the floor (0.06 per cell of height; the top stays under the rails). **Longitudinal bracing** joins a bent to the next bent along the curve (the piece's forward joint, the `t1` side, when that piece gets a support too — the floor search is the same helper, so a neighbour's bent is found without building it): a stringer on each side at every tie level both bents reach (the cap stringer follows the grade), and one diagonal per bay per side, alternating direction bay to bay. Each bay is drawn once, from the earlier piece; laying or removing the next piece re-draws this one (`_refresh_rail_neighbours`, the 3×3×3 neighbourhood) so the stringers appear and vanish with it. The skip rules are unchanged (inverted pieces, a piece within two cells above another piece of the same curve, nothing solid within 24 cells). Struts are `MeshInstance3D` boxes named `Post` / `Post2`, `Pad`, `Tie`, `Diagonal`, `Stringer` (numbered after the first) under the `Support` node; one `BoxMesh` is shared per strut size (`_support_meshes`), so a 40-cell climb 12 high is on the order of a thousand instances. T183 (gate); T178 `coaster-climb.png` and T181 `coaster-supports.png` are framed to show the bents.

## The heading follows the aim (owner playtest 2026-09-20)

Owner: "I want where I place the ghost to be the starting point of what I build … the item should start from where I place and extend towards its rotated direction, or be dragged from that spot." Every coaster tool's press (`InteractionService.begin_drag_place` for loop, climb, curve, bend, cross, switch) now sets the build orientation to the player's facing (`facing_quarters`: the rotation whose `switch_along` best matches the horizontal look direction), so the aimed cell is the entry and the piece heads away from the player; the ghost is seen from its entry. W / R still turn the piece afterwards. While Shift is held: a **climb** or **loop** heads along the drag (`_span_quarters`: the dominant horizontal axis of aim − entry, once the drag is 2+ cells), a **bend / cross** dragged 2+ cells *behind* its entry flips to head that way (the forward distance stays positive), and the **curve** keeps its pressed heading (the drag's angle picks the sweep, as before). The plain rail line is unchanged (it lays toward the aim already). Creative (the sandbox): dismantling a piece or mining a block with a full pack discards the drop instead of refusing (`WorkstationService.creative`, `InteractionService.creative`). The sandbox resets the Curve tool to 90° / radius 4 after its U-turn demo. The pause menu is two columns (game left, drills right) so it fits the screen. T182 (gate).

## Undo (owner playtest 2026-09-20)

Owner: "I need an undo feature because when I place something big improperly, it takes forever to chop it down." **U** or **Ctrl+Z** (`GameSession.undo_last_placement` → `InteractionService.undo_last`) reverses the newest placement. Every placement records an undo entry (`InteractionService._undo`, `UNDO_DEPTH` 12): `commit_drag_place` (all lay tools and block drags) and `try_place_item` (single pieces / blocks) snapshot the station ids, the voxels of the cells they may touch (the planned cells and their auto-clear lists) and the pack's counts before, and record what changed after - the stations created, the voxels that changed (before / after) and the pack's net change per item. Undo removes the created pieces that still exist (`WorkstationService.try_dismantle(..., refund = false)` - no per-piece refund, since curve pieces are `rail_loop` records paid for with the tool's item), restores each changed voxel that still holds what the placement wrote, hands back the items the placement cost and takes back its drops (mined blocks); when the pack cannot take the whole refund, what fits is refunded (creative packs are full anyway). An active ghost is cancelled first. The stack is per session (not saved) and ignores removals (chopping is not undoable). T184 (gate).

## One track style (owner playtest 2026-09-21)

Owner: "We have been mixing older rail blocks and the old Rail Slope and the old Rail Switch. I would like them updated to match the newer 'curvable' pieces. Also, is it possible that we can smooth these rail sections? It would be nice if curves transitioned smoothly." Every track piece now draws with one renderer: `GameSession._add_track_run` lays a run of sections through world points - two iron rails at ±0.22 (gauge 0.44), 0.10 square, an oak tie and a stone spine per section on the side away from a `lean` point (below the rails for flat track, outside the ring for a loop) - and each section node carries the meta `rail_length` for the automation's joint probe. The old rail block (oak deck, stone corner posts, gold studs, centre plate) and the old slope (deck, posts) are gone.

- **Plain `rail`** (`_build_rail_visual(parent, record)`, also flat `rail_loop` pieces): from the ride point a run to the meeting point with every joined neighbour. **Exactly two neighbours at right angles on the cell's edges** draw a **quarter arc of radius 0.5 centred on the inner corner** (six sections, no centre plate) so a plain-rail corner is round like a curve piece. Three or four neighbours: straight arms and a small iron `JunctionPlate` (0.54×0.06×0.54). A diagonal neighbour (the classic loop's 45° step): a straight to the cell corner. No neighbour: a straight along the placement rotation; one neighbour mirrors it. Collision unchanged (the rail block's box; the player walks on rails, carts ride by the service).
- **`rail_slope`** (`_build_rail_slope_visual(parent, record)`): the run along the 45° line through the ride point (anchor + (0.5, 1.05, 0.5)) from the **low edge at this level's rail top (+0.55)** to the **high edge at the next level's rail top (+1.55)**, five sections (a tie every ~0.3), so plain rails on both levels meet it flush at their own edge midpoint - no bend needed. `_slope_rail_end(slope, from_cell)` gives either end. (`CoasterRails.slope_rail_corner` / `SLOPE_RAIL_TOP` 1.04 remains the classic ring's tangent height; fit C's 0.5 lift brings the ring up to the drawn high end, and the ring renderer still bridges the lift.) A short trestle bent stands near the slope's high end (`_support_bent` now serves every track id; ground-level rails are skipped by its 0.5 minimum height).
- **`rail_switch`** (`_build_rail_switch_visual`): the rail renderer along the piece's ride line - the middles' ride point is a quarter cell onto the diagonal (`switch_mid_shift`), so entry → mid_a → mid_b → exit reads as one track shifting a lane through the meeting points; the middles' node keeps the name `SwitchRails` (T168). Old saves with standalone switch pieces draw the same way.
- **Meeting points** (`_track_meeting_point(record, other)`): a slope's rail end on the other piece's side, else halfway between the two ride points (the shared edge's midpoint for two flat rails, the cell corner for a diagonal step, the shifted midpoint beside a switch middle). Both pieces of a joint call it with the roles swapped, so their runs end at one point. `_build_loop_track_visual` (curve and ring pieces) uses the same helper for any neighbour that is not on its own curve / circle; the classic ring keeps its lifted-corner bridge to a slope. Ride geometry (`CoasterRails.ride_point`, `CoasterCartService`) is untouched - visuals only.
- **T185** (gate): on a levelled plate a plain-rail L corner, a rail-slope-rail run, a radius-4 rail-curve-rail run and a diameter-6 rail-loop-rail true loop: the corner has no `RailArms` / `JunctionPlate` and ≥ 5 sections, no rail or slope carries a stone-post or deck box, and every joined pair's `rail_end_gap` (the smallest distance between one piece's rail ends and the other's) is < 0.02 (measured 1.5e-5 over 98 joints). T163's render now frames the slope run and a plain-rail corner beside the loop; `coaster-smooth.png`, `coaster-climb.png`, `coaster-curves.png` and `coaster-supports.png` regenerate with the plain rails in the shared style.

## Snap to a track end (owner playtest 2026-09-21)

Owner: "I need a way to know if I am touching another piece when I want to lay a track. Currently, the ghosts do not snap to an endpoint. What I want is to give the piece it is going to attach to a color highlight, like yellow, indicating that a connect was made and ready to snap these pieces together."

- **Open ends** (`CoasterRails.open_ends(tracks)`, `tracks` = `track_records`): every place a next piece would join the laid track - `{cell, next, along, instance_id}`, `next` the free joint cell, `along` the unit direction out of the piece toward it (a curve piece's end tangent via `curve_end_heading`, so a 45° end reports its diagonal). A joint whose cell holds a track is not open; a slope end counts once (its same-level joint; the one-lower alternative is the same end, and an end already joined through that alternative is closed). A plain rail has two **implied** ends: straight on from its one connected neighbour, or both cells along its placement axis when it stands alone; a rail joined on two sides (a run or a corner) has none. Loop pieces placed by hand (no recorded joints) report none.
- **The snap** (`InteractionService.find_snap` / `_follow_anchor`, `SNAP_REACH` 1.6): while any track ghost follows the aim (loop, climb, curve, bend / cross, lane switcher, and the plain rail line at its press), an aimed entry within 1.6 cells horizontally (same level or one up / down) of an open end's `next` cell moves the entry onto that cell, turns the piece to leave the end (`placement_rotation_quarters = facing_quarters(along)`; W / R are overridden while snapped) and records `drag_state().snap = {instance_id, cell, next}` (`{}` when none). Ends whose `along` is diagonal (a 45° / 135° curve end) are never snapped to - a curve pressed on their diagonal cell continues them as before. The nearest end wins. Holding Shift keeps the entry (and the snap) where it was, as before. A rail line snaps its entry at the press; its end follows the aim.
- **The highlight** (`GameSession._add_snap_hint`): a translucent yellow glow (`SnapHint`, a 1.04×0.7×1.04 box at the piece's ride point, alpha 0.45, emissive) under the drag preview root - never under the piece's body - on the piece named by `snap`; it goes when the snap clears or the drag ends. The feedback line "Snapped to the track end — release to join." (`TRACK_SNAPPED`) shows once per snap.
- **The join**: nothing new - a snapped ghost's first piece sits on the free joint cell and records the piece behind it (`before` for curves, adjacency for rails), so release joins the run.
- Fixed in passing: `update_drag_place` for a climb fell through to the block-drag tail and answered `NO_DRAG` instead of the drag state; it now returns the state like the other tools.
- **T187** (gate): three plain rails heading +x on a levelled plate report exactly two open ends (west of the first, east of the last); a Rail Curve right-press aimed one cell beside and one short of the run's free cell snaps (entry = that cell, rotation 1, `snap.instance_id` = the last rail, `SnapHint` on the ghost); aimed three cells away the snap and hint clear; aimed back it re-snaps and release lays the curve (`CURVE_PLACED`) chained to the run, and a cart from the first rail rides through the curve past its exit.
